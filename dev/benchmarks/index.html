Benchmarks

Two machines:

MachineGPUCPU
WorkstationNVIDIA RTX A6000, SM clock locked at 1200 MHz, CUDA runtime 13.4, driver 595.91AMD Threadripper PRO 5995WX, 128 threads
LaptopApple M3 Pro, through MetalM3 Pro, 6 performance and 6 efficiency cores

VkFFT runs against the FFT each device already has: cuFFT through CUDA.jl on the A6000, MPSGraph through Metal.jl on the M3 Pro, and FFTW on the CPU. On the A6000 VkFFT runs through CUDA and through OpenCL. On the laptop OpenCL is pocl, on the CPU.

Each sweep runs five times, each in a fresh process. A sample queues a batch of transforms, about 5 ms of work, and waits once, so the times are what a loop of mul! calls costs per transform. A time is the median across the five runs, and the bands and whiskers span them. Transforms that overwrite their input, in place and irfft, cannot be batched, so their samples are one call plus one wait.

The harness is in benchmarks/ in the repository. benchmarks/run.jl measures and benchmarks/plot.jl draws these figures from its output.

Speed against cuFFT, MPSGraph and FFTW

Above 1x VkFFT is faster. Over every case measured, VkFFT beats cuFFT in 186 of 235 by a geometric mean of 1.29x, and MPSGraph in 175 of 204 by 1.47x. Prime lengths, which run through Bluestein's algorithm, are where VkFFT leads most.

cuFFT is faster from $2^{15}$ to $2^{18}$ in single precision, and MPSGraph at $2^{17}$ and $2^{18}$. Past $2^{20}$ both stay within about 10% of VkFFT.

On a CPU, FFTW is faster than VkFFT through pocl at every length here. Use VkFFTOpenCL on a CPU when you need the same code to run without a GPU, not for speed.

Time per transform

The flat stretch on the left is the cost of queueing one transform, about 3 μs for VkFFT on CUDA and 11 to 15 μs on Metal. MPSGraph sits at about 21 μs.

The GPU overtakes the faster of the two FFTW runs at 8192 points on the A6000 and at 16384 on the M3 Pro. Below that, keep small transforms on the CPU.

Lengths around 4096

Both libraries run 4096 points in 6 to 7 μs. Neighbouring lengths cost depending on how they factor, not on their distance from 4096. A length with a large prime factor goes through Bluestein's algorithm, where VkFFT takes 23 to 26 μs and cuFFT 29 to 40 μs.

CUDA and OpenCL on one A6000

The same A6000 three ways. VkFFT on CUDA over VkFFT on OpenCL is the cost of the API, since the kernels are the same. VkFFT over cuFFT, both on CUDA, is the difference between the two kernel generators.

The API costs nothing at most lengths. The exceptions are $2^{14}$, where OpenCL takes twice as long, and most primes, where OpenCL is faster.

Speed by transform shape

Geometric mean speedup per shape, in single precision. The lead is largest on 1D complex transforms. On rfft, 2D, 3D and batched transforms it shrinks to within about 15% either way. The irfft bars are single calls, see above.

Accuracy

Relative L2 error against a higher-precision reference, in units of the precision's epsilon. On powers of two every implementation stays within a few epsilons. In single precision VkFFT's error is about twice cuFFT's, and in double precision it is below it. VkFFT computes half precision in single and only stores it in half, so its half-precision error sits below one half-precision epsilon.

Uniform input hides the error at prime lengths, because its spectrum is dominated by one bin. A chirp spreads it out. On a chirp, VkFFT in double precision through pocl grows to 47 epsilons at 65521 points, against 2.2 on the A6000's OpenCL driver, and MPSGraph in single precision reaches 141.

Tuning

tune=true changes nothing on almost every shape. It pays off on large 2D transforms: 1.57x at 1024² on Metal, and 1.30x at 2048² and 1.63x at 4096² through OpenCL on the A6000. See Tuning.

The tuner's 16 candidates on Metal, relative to the fastest, for the shape tuning helps most and for one it does not help. At 1024² one value of coalesced_memory is 1.6x faster than the rest. At $2^{23}$ every candidate is within 1% of the best.
